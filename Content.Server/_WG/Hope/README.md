# Hope

What answers the Coda's song. The Choir sings the sector apart; the Sultan's hymns hold it together. This module is
the foundation for that side of the setting, starting with her hymns.

- **Hymn sources:** any entity with a `WGHymnSource` sings hymns to everyone within its `range` (metres), whoever
  they are. `source` picks the pool: every `wgHymn` prototype with the same `source`. `gap` is the quiet between
  hymns. The first is `WGAltarSultan`, the altar of the Blessed Figure aboard Helios, which sings the Sultan's hymns
  within 18 m. To put her altar elsewhere, map a `WGAltarSultan`; to make a different kind of source, give any entity
  a `WGHymnSource` with a new `source` and add `wgHymn` prototypes for it.
- **Hearing them:** twice a second `WGHymnSourceSystem` (server) tells each player the nearest source in reach and
  how near (`WGHymnListenerComponent`, sent only to its owner), so a source is heard across its whole range.
  `WGHymnSystem` (client) then plays a hymn from the pool, swelling as they come near and fading as they walk away.
- **Music gives way:** while a hymn has the listener, ambient music fades out and is held off under the `Hymn` music
  pause reason (`ContentAudioSystem.Hymn.cs`). A hymn gives way to combat music rather than holding it off, and an
  internet sound or the soundtrack mute silences it.
- **Once the Coda's song is in (#857):** it goes quiet whenever music is held by anything but itself, so near a hymn
  source the Choir will not be heard. A Lattice band's own piece should then wait for a hymn as it waits for the Choir.

The Sultan's hymns are in D major, led by one woman over an unbroken ison, and every one moves from dark to light.
They are rendered by `make_sultan_hymn.py`, kept outside the repo with the Choir's generator; `--list` names them and
`--variant <name> --out <file>` renders one. The game copies are re-encoded at Vorbis quality 4.
