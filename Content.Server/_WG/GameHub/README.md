# GameHub

Games that run on the player's own client, and what they share with the server: their downloads, the sound nearby
players hear, the leaderboard and cabinet prizes. Every PDA gets the Gamehub program (`WGGameHubCartridge`), which lists
each `wgGame` prototype to download, play and compare scores on. Games also run on arcade cabinets, such as `WGDoomArcade`.

**Downloads.** A game's data sits under `Resources/GameData`, which clients don't ship (`Content.Packaging/ClientPackaging.cs`).
`WGGameDownloadSystem` streams it over the transfer channel, rate-limited because most servers share it with the game
connection (`wg.gamehub.download_rate`, `wg.gamehub.max_downloads`). The client's `WGGameDataManager` keeps it in user
data with its version, so a player only downloads it again when it changes.

**Nearby sound.** The playing client reports each sound and music track. `WGGameHubSystem` passes sounds to players in
PVS, capped by `wg.gamehub.max_noise_per_second`, and puts the track on `WGGameBroadcastComponent`. Bystanders haven't
downloaded the game, so every client ships a small noise pack of its sound and music lumps, and renders the music itself.
A row of cabinets stays bearable: each client plays only the nearest game's music and at most
`WGGameHubSystem.MaxNearbyNoises` sounds at once, and active games share the volume.

**Scores and prizes.** The game runs on the client, so a modified client can lie. A finished level is checked for a real
level number, a plausible time (`wg.gamehub.min_level_seconds`) and a player within reach of the cabinet or PDA. A
player's board score is the sum of their best score per level, so replaying a level can't inflate it. Cabinets pay
tickets, once per level per round and up to `wg.gamehub.max_prizes_per_round`; PDAs pay nothing.

To add a game: a `wgGame` prototype, and a window opened from `Launch` in the client's `WGGameHubSystem`. The block game
and Space Villain aren't listed because they run on the server, tied to their cabinets.
