# JoinQueue

Lets players wait in a queue when the server is at its soft player cap (`game.soft_max_players`), instead of being
refused. A queued player is connected but not in the game, and sees a screen with how full the server is, their place
in the queue, how long they have waited and a rough estimate of the wait. When a slot opens the first in line joins
the lobby or the game as usual.

While they wait they can play Freedoom or Freedoom Phase 2 from buttons on the screen (DoomArcade, with no cabinet, so
nobody else hears it and finished levels win nothing; the window closes when they are let in), and the moproach from
the connect screen wanders about asking for spills, mopping up any the player clicks onto the background.

- Admins who bypass the cap and players who already reached the lobby this round skip the queue, as they skip the cap.
- A round restart leaves waiting players out of the lobby, so they can't ready up past the queue. Admins see
  "waiting in the join queue" when a player starts waiting and "joined from the join queue" when let in.
- A queued player who disconnects keeps their place for `wg.join_queue.hold_seconds` (120). A slot that opens while
  they are away goes to the next connected player, and their place stays.
- The queue holds `wg.join_queue.max_size` (50) players; past that the server refuses the connection as before.
  `wg.join_queue.enabled` turns the queue off, and everyone waiting is then let in.
- Waiting players don't count towards the cap or the lobby's player count. The count the hub and launcher show
  includes them, so a full server with a queue reads over its cap (120/100); `wg.join_queue.count_in_status` off
  shows only players in the game.
- The engine's `net.max_connections` must be above the cap plus the queue size, or the engine refuses the connection
  before the queue sees it.

`JoinQueueManager` (server) decides at `SessionStatus.Connected` whether a player joins or waits, where `GameTicker`
would call `JoinGame`. It lets players in when someone disconnects, when the cap changes, and on its tick. The order,
held places and the estimate are `JoinQueueLine`; the estimate is the pace of the last ten openings (players let in together count as one) times the player's
place, and is unknown until two slots have opened. Each change sends every waiting player a `MsgJoinQueueUpdate`, and
at least every ten seconds.

The client's `JoinQueueManager` switches to `JoinQueueState` on the first message. `JoinQueueScreen` draws it in the
join console's look (`_WG/JoinProgress`) and counts the timers between messages. When the run level reaches `InGame`
the player was let in: the screen says so until the lobby or the game takes over, and the window flashes.
